[00:01:40] Ana: A funnel has an end. A loop feeds itself. The practical test I use with teams: draw your
growth model on a whiteboard — if the arrows eventually point back to the start, you have a loop; if they
just terminate at "purchase," you have a funnel with a growth team bolted on.

[00:07:10] Ana: The most durable loops I've seen are content loops, where user-generated output (a public
profile, a shared doc, a published report) is both the product value and the acquisition channel at the
same time. That's structurally different from a referral program, which is a funnel tactic wearing a
loop's clothing — it still requires a separate campaign to keep running.

[00:14:05] Ana: A tactical takeaway: for every loop you design, explicitly write down the "reinjection"
step — the moment a new user, having received value, is exposed to the action that pulls in the next
person. If you can't point to that specific screen or moment, you don't actually have a loop yet, you
have a hope.
